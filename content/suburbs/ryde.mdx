localNotes:
  - heading: A timber subfloor changes what a bathroom costs
    body: >-
      In a house with a timber subfloor we can usually move drainage without
      cutting concrete, which saves money. What it adds back is joist repair
      and a new sheeting substrate, because the floor has to be stiff enough
      to meet current deflection limits before anything gets tiled. On balance
      it is roughly a wash, but the two jobs feel completely different and are
      priced differently.
  - heading: The fall toward the river
    body: >-
      The land drops toward the Parramatta River on the southern side of the
      suburb. On the steeper streets, a rear extension means retaining,
      drainage and often a suspended floor rather than a slab. The cost sits
      almost entirely below the finished floor level, which makes it feel
      invisible and makes it easy to underestimate.
  - heading: Ryde's own controls sit on top of the state codes
    body: >-
      The City of Ryde development control plan applies additional
      requirements beyond the state codes, and Ryde is one of the stricter
      councils in the area on side setbacks for additions. Older houses close
      to a boundary are the most common reason a Ryde job needs a development
      application rather than a complying development certificate.
faqs:
  - question: How much does a bathroom renovation cost in Ryde?
    answer: >-
      Our bathrooms have fallen between $28,000 and $55,000 including GST for a
      full strip out back to frame, rewaterproofed and refitted. In this
      suburb the biggest single variable is what we find when the old
      waterproofing comes up, which in houses of this age is often nothing at
      all behind the tile bed.
  - question: Do I need council approval for a bathroom or kitchen renovation?
    answer: >-
      Not for planning purposes, provided you are not changing the building
      footprint. The work still has to comply with the Building Code, wet area
      waterproofing has to be certified, and licensed trades have to issue
      their own compliance certificates. Those we handle.
  - question: Which council covers Ryde?
    answer: >-
      City of Ryde, which also covers West Ryde, Eastwood and the surrounding
      suburbs on that side of the area.
  - question: Do you work on houses older than the 1960s?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Our focus is 1960s to 1990s stock because that is most of what is
      here, but interwar cottages are common in Ryde and we renovate them
      regularly. They come with their own issues — no damp course in some
      cases, lath and plaster, and stumps rather than piers — and we price
      them with that in mind.
