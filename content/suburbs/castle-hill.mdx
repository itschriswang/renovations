localNotes:
  - heading: Large lots make complying development straightforward
    body: >-
      Site coverage, floor space ratio and setbacks are rarely the limiting
      factor here. A high proportion of Castle Hill additions can be approved
      by a private certifier in weeks. The constraints that do bite are height
      on the sloping blocks and, very commonly, trees.
  - heading: Tree preservation is the usual complication
    body: >-
      The Hills Shire protects a wide range of species and sizes. On a large
      treed lot the practical effect is that the position of the extension is
      often decided by root protection zones rather than by the plan. An
      arborist report early avoids drawing something that cannot be built.
  - heading: Secondary dwellings work well on lots this size
    body: >-
      Lot sizes here comfortably exceed the minimum for a secondary dwelling
      under the state provisions, and side access is usually good enough that
      machinery can reach the back. That combination makes Castle Hill one of
      the better suburbs in the area for a granny flat.
faqs:
  - question: Can I build a granny flat in Castle Hill?
    answer: >-
      Most Castle Hill lots comfortably meet the minimum lot size for a
      secondary dwelling under the state provisions, and many can be approved
      as complying development. The practical constraints are usually access
      for machinery and the location of the sewer main rather than the
      planning controls.
  - question: How much does a granny flat cost?
    answer: >-
      Our secondary dwellings have fallen between $180,000 and $280,000
      including GST for a detached build up to 60 square metres. Site access is
      the single biggest variable — no side access can add a very large amount
      to the figure.
  - question: Which council covers Castle Hill?
    answer: >-
      The Hills Shire.
  - question: Do I need approval to remove a tree before building?
    answer: >-
      Almost certainly, unless it is on the exempt species list or below the
      protected size. Removal is not approved simply because the tree is in the
      way of a proposed extension, so it is better to design around it or to
      establish early whether removal is achievable.
