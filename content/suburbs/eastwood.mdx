localNotes:
  - heading: The suburb is split across two councils
    body: >-
      Eastwood straddles the boundary between the City of Ryde and the City of
      Parramatta. Which one applies to you is a matter of which side of the
      line your title sits on, not which side of the shops you live on. It
      changes your development control plan, your fees and your timeframes, so
      we confirm it from the title rather than the postcode.
  - heading: Mature trees are the most common design constraint
    body: >-
      The tree cover that makes the suburb pleasant also sits in a lot of
      build areas. Both councils protect trees by species and size, and the
      root protection zone of a large tree can restrict excavation well beyond
      the canopy. An arborist report early is cheaper than a redesign late.
  - heading: Earlier additions are often the weak point
    body: >-
      A 1990s addition on a separate slab is very common here and it matters
      enormously if you are considering a second storey. It also matters for
      drainage, because those additions frequently discharged stormwater in
      ways that would not be approved now.

    answer: >-
      Usually, but the tree shapes the design rather than the other way around.

      may limit where footings can go. Removing a protected tree needs consent
      and is not granted simply because it is inconvenient.
