localNotes:
  - heading: The bushfire attack level assessment comes first
    body: >-
      If your property is mapped as bushfire prone, a bushfire attack level
      assessment determines the construction standard you have to build to.
      It affects glazing, external cladding, decking, screening of openings
      and the subfloor. At the higher levels it adds a material amount to the
      cost of an extension, and it is not something that can be value
      engineered out later. Get the assessment before the design, not after.
  - heading: Tree protection affects excavation, not just removal
    body: >-
      Hornsby protects a broad range of trees, and the root protection zone of
      a large tree restricts where you can excavate well beyond the drip line.
      On a treed block this frequently determines where a new footing can go,
      which determines where the extension can go.
  - heading: Slope and subfloor access
    body: >-
      Many houses here step down the block on a timber subfloor. That is good
      news for services access and bad news for level changes, and it makes
      matching a new floor level to an existing one a designed detail rather
      than an assumption.
faqs:
  - question: Is my Pennant Hills property bushfire prone?
    answer: >-
      A large share of the suburb is mapped as bushfire prone land. It is
      recorded on the planning certificate for your property, which we obtain
      at feasibility stage before any design work begins.
  - question: How much does bushfire compliance add to an extension?
    answer: >-
      It depends on the assessed attack level. At the lower levels the impact
      is modest. At the higher levels the glazing, cladding and screening
      requirements are substantial and need to be in the budget from the start.
      We price it explicitly rather than burying it in an allowance.
  - question: Which council covers Pennant Hills?
    answer: >-
      Hornsby Shire. Hornsby's determination timeframes are typically at the
      longer end of the councils we work across, partly because of the volume
      of bushfire and tree referrals.
  - question: Can I still get complying development approval on a bushfire prone block?
    answer: >-
      Sometimes, depending on the assessed attack level and whether the design
      meets the relevant standards. Above a certain level, complying
      development is not available and a development application is required.
      We establish this at feasibility.
