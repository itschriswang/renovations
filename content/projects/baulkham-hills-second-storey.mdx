## What the house was

A 1985 project home on an 812 square metre lot, four bedrooms, two bathrooms,
with a family room added at the back around 1998. The owners had three children
and had been extending sideways into every available room for a decade. They
had already had two builders tell them a full second storey was
straightforward. It was not, and the difference was a $95,000 slab problem that
neither of the earlier quotes had looked for.

## The engineer's inspection is not optional

We will not price a second storey without an engineer physically inspecting the
existing footings, which means excavating an inspection pit at two or three
points around the house and opening a section of wall. It costs a few thousand
dollars and half a day of mess.

On this job it changed the entire scope. On roughly one job in five it finds
something that changes the design. On the rest it confirms the house can carry
the load, which is worth knowing with certainty before you sign a contract for
half a million dollars.

## Living in it, and then not

The family stayed for the first eleven weeks and moved into a rental for
fourteen. We plan this properly at contract stage rather than leaving it to be
discovered — the dates when the house is uninhabitable are on the construction
programme from the start, so the rental can be booked at a sensible price
rather than in a panic.

Budget for it as part of the project. Fourteen weeks of rent in this area is a
material number and leaving it out of the planning is how a project that came
in on price still feels like it went over.
