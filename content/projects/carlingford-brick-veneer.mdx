  body: >-
    The house faced north to the street, which put the only usable outdoor
    space at the back and the only sensible location for a new living area
    directly on top of a Sydney Water sewer main running diagonally across the
    rear yard. The main was 1.9 metres deep and served eleven properties
    upstream, so it was not a line anyone was going to abandon. Relocating it
    was quoted at a figure that would have consumed a quarter of the
    construction budget before a single brick was laid, and it carried a
    twelve week approval risk we could not price with any confidence.

decision:
  heading: We built over it rather than around it or away from it
  body: >-
    Sydney Water permits building over a main under a build-over application
    where the structure is designed so the main stays accessible and the
    building loads are carried clear of it. That meant a pier and beam
    subfloor instead of a slab, with bored piers either side of the easement
    and a beam spanning it, and an access shaft retained inside the new floor.
    The alternative designs both cost the client something real: pushing the
    room sideways would have taken the northern light the whole project
    existed to capture, and shortening it would have left a room too narrow
    for a table and a lounge together. The pier and beam floor added
    approximately eleven per cent to the structural cost and preserved the
    plan. We put all three options in front of the owners with the numbers
    attached and they chose this one in about ten minutes.

wentWrong:
  heading: The bored piers hit rock two metres short of design depth
  body: >-
    The geotechnical report was based on two boreholes. Neither of them was
    where the third pier ended up going. At 2.4 metres the auger hit sandstone
    that the report had put at 4.5 metres, which sounds like good news and is
    not — the pier design assumed a friction pile in clay, and a short pier
    bearing on rock behaves differently enough that the engineer would not
    certify it as drawn.
  resolution: >-
    We stopped that pier, left the rig on site rather than demobilising it,
    and had the engineer back on site the same afternoon. He redesigned three
    piers as rock sockets, which needed a different bit and one extra day of
    rig hire. Total delay was four working days, three of which we absorbed
    into float we had already built into the programme for exactly this kind
    of thing on a piered job.
  costToClient: >-
    Nothing. The fixed price contract carried the ground risk, which is the
    entire reason we investigate before we price rather than writing a
    provisional sum and hoping. The client was told about it the day it
    happened, because they would rather hear it from us than notice the
    programme slipping.

## What the house was

A single storey brick veneer built in 1974 on a regular 695 square metre lot,
north to the street. Three bedrooms, one bathroom, a kitchen closed off from
everything, and a living room that got no direct sun after ten in the morning.
The owners had been in it eleven years and had two teenagers who had run out of
room to be anywhere other than their bedrooms.

The house itself was in good order. Original concrete tile roof with about a
decade left in it, no movement in the walls, and a subfloor that was dry. None
of the money needed to go into repair, which is rarer than you would think in
this stock and made the budget go considerably further.

The piering premium is visible in that first line. On the same house without
the sewer main, structure would have been closer to 24 per cent and the
contract would have been roughly $30,000 lower.

## What we would do differently

Order the geotechnical investigation with three boreholes rather than two on
any job where the structural solution depends on pier depth. The extra
borehole was around $900. The redesign, the extra rig day and the four day
delay cost us appreciably more than that, and we carried it because that is
what a fixed price means. It is now standard on every piered job we price.
